# What your AI subscriptions really cost

*Condensed from [AI subscription costs: calculate your true monthly spend](https://whizi.io/resources/ai-subscription-costs/) on whizi.io.*

Most AI subscription costs do not start with a budget decision. They start with a small exception: one person needs a better writing model, another wants a coding assistant, someone signs up for an image tool, and the founder keeps a separate account for research. Each charge feels reasonable on its own. The problem appears when the team realises it is paying for the same core capability several times. Three $20 chat plans on one card is $60 a month, $720 a year, for tools that overlap on most everyday prompts.

## The worksheet

List every paid plan from your billing pages, convert annual charges to a monthly figure, and tag each tool by the job it does. Then separate active spend from duplicated spend.

| Tool or plan | Primary job | Monthly cost | Used weekly? | Overlap risk | Keep, cut, or test |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| ChatGPT Plus | Writing and research | $20 | Yes | High if Claude Pro covers the same work | Test consolidation |
| Claude Pro | Drafting and code review | $20, or $17 billed annually | Yes | High if ChatGPT Plus is already on the card | Keep one, not both |
| AI coding tool | Debugging and tests | From your billing page | Yes | Medium if developers still need IDE-native help | Keep or partial cut |
| Google AI Pro | Long documents, Gmail and Docs | $19.99 | Rarely | High if a multi-model workspace handles docs | Cut or consolidate |
| Perplexity Pro | Sources and synthesis | $20 | Yes | Medium if citations are essential | Compare workflow quality |

Prices are United States list prices as of August 2026. Verify each on the vendor site when you fill in your own row.

## What the pattern usually means

| Scenario | What it usually means | Best next step |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One person pays for several chatbots | They are comparing models manually | Consolidate into one place and run prompts side by side |
| A team has many unused seats | Buying outpaced adoption | Remove inactive seats before buying more |
| Users copy outputs between tools | The workflow is fragmented | Move the workflow into a shared workspace |
| A tool is used once a month | The plan may be convenience spend | Test whether one workspace covers the occasional task |
| A tool is used daily for a specialised job | It may deserve to stay | Keep it unless a trial proves replacement is safe |

## The decision rule

If two plans on the card do the same job for the same person, cancel one this month and see what breaks. Usually nothing does.
